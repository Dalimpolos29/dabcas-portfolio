## The problem

The client tracked inventory across three warehouses in a shared spreadsheet. Two people edited it
at once most days, stock counts drifted from reality, and reconciling the difference took a full
afternoon every week.

They didn't need a warehouse management suite. They needed one accurate number per item, visible to
everyone, updated as things moved.

## What I built

A multi-tenant web application with a Postgres schema modelled around stock movements rather than
stock levels — every change is an append-only event, and the current level is derived from it. That
made the audit trail free and eliminated the class of bug where two concurrent edits overwrite each
other.

- **Movement ledger** as the single source of truth, with derived level views
- **Role-based access** so warehouse staff can record movements but not edit history
- **Offline-tolerant scanning** on the warehouse floor, syncing when connectivity returns
- **Weekly reconciliation report** generated automatically instead of assembled by hand

## Decisions worth explaining

**Event sourcing over a mutable quantity column.** A single `quantity` field looks simpler until two
devices write to it in the same second. Recording movements and deriving the total removed the race
condition entirely and gave the client the audit history they hadn't thought to ask for.

**Server Components for the read paths.** Most of the app is reading data, not mutating it. Rendering
those views on the server kept the client bundle small enough to stay responsive on the low-end
Android tablets already in the warehouses.

## Outcome

Reconciliation dropped from roughly six hours a week to under twenty minutes. The system has been
running in production since launch, and the client has since added a fourth location without any
code changes.
